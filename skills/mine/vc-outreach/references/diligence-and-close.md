# Diligence & Close — staged disclosure, data room, wire

Diligence is a hook built in advance, not a hurdle survived after the term sheet (Dreamit). The investor-side sequence to plan around (VC Unlocked/500): **commercial diligence pre-term-sheet by the investing team** (demo, market, references, background checks — "you wouldn't take on a renter without a background check"); expensive legal/tax/technical diligence **only after a signed term sheet**; **the lead does most of it** and followers piggyback. A non-lead demanding a full legal data room pre-term-sheet is out of protocol — push back politely.

## Staged disclosure (three gates — Underscore)

1. **First meeting** — short deck only; the job is harvesting questions and reservations.
2. **Partner pitch** — long-form deck + entry-level data room, *shaped by the objections gate 1 surfaced*.
3. **Post-term-sheet** — full legal diligence, counsel-led, enhanced-access room.

Keep follow-up deliverables in a separate folder and reuse across investors asking the same question; a running FAQ document compounds the same way.

## The data room

**Entry level** (interested investors, partner-pitch onward): cover letter guiding the reader (customize per investor; optionally a short reusable investment memo) · expanded deck, hosted with view tracking · org chart + planned hires, leadership bios · product overview, roadmap, **demo videos**, customer-testimonial videos · financial summary deck — historical actuals, 2-year projections, KPIs (**never a live model** — "throwing a ton of data at VCs without context leads to misinterpretation" — Lyman) · fully diluted cap table (pool, SAFEs, notes, warrants) + prior instruments · 1–2-page executive summary · **master index**: numbered request → file path → status (Ready / In Progress / N/A) → owner — cuts response time up to 50% (Ascent CFO, 2026).

**Enhanced level** (post-term-sheet only): incorporation docs + amendments, bylaws, good standing, 24 months of board minutes · stockholder/option-holder lists with dates and prices, vesting + acceleration · **IP assignments from every employee, contractor, and founder** (the classic landmine), IP inventory, third-party/OSS licenses + SBOM · material agreements, debt, liens, insurance · top 10–20 customer contracts (redacted) + commercial-terms summary (billing, auto-renew, termination, MFN, indemnities) · employment list with title/salary/classification, change-of-control and severance terms · litigation and regulatory · security/compliance pack offered proactively (SOC 2, privacy).

Hygiene: per-investor folders with access revoked when a process ends (the control that substitutes for the NDA no VC signs) · read-only + watermark + expiry + view logs · `YYYYMMDD_Topic_v1.2.pdf` naming, no stale creation dates in filenames · redact PII and customer names; pending patents = filing receipts, not full applications · refresh monthly during an active raise · answer data-room follow-ups **within 24 hours** ("wasted time kills deal momentum!") · get investor questions in writing beforehand, then take the call rehearsed.

Forecast discipline: share the same forecast the board sees; never adjust forecasts, metrics, or plan mid-raise — "dramatically changing critical information in the middle of a fundraise is a clear sign you don't have a handle on your business" (Orsillo, Underscore). "I assume you run your company like you run your deal room" (Dulude, Underscore).

## Reference calls — both directions

**Outbound (theirs about the company):** choose references for accuracy, not enthusiasm; brief them on the process, never script them; pre-flag any mixed reference so the context arrives from the founder first. Ration live customer references — pre-recorded testimonial videos serve the curious; live calls are reserved for investors at real commitment (the escalation ladder in `meetings.md`: references ≈ 25% likely to fund). Supplying named references proactively collapses diligence from weeks to ~24 hours.

**Inbound (the founder's, about the investor):** "Thou shalt do investor references" (Northflank). Never accept the cherry-picked founder — request or research the full portfolio and pick independently, including someone whose company struggled. Four questions: how do they handle conflict? do they show up prepared or multitask? where were they most helpful? what one thing would you change about them?

## Term sheet → wire

- "You don't have a term sheet until you have an actual term sheet" — verbal commits are air. Once real paper arrives, route the economics and clause scan to **vc-strategy's deal-terms reference**; this file owns the logistics.
- Tell every other live investor a term sheet exists; ask what's left in their process. Insist on ≥3 business days to consider; expiration dates are mostly theater — "in five years as a VC, I never saw a firm pull an active term sheet because it hit its expiration date" (Northflank) — but never go silent; ask for extensions explicitly.
- **Don't stop selling.** "Your round is not closed until the funds wire" — set quick, reasonable wire dates and drive to them.
- Mechanics and costs (priced round): lead's document review ~1 month · term sheet (1–2 pp) → SPA + ancillaries (10–20 pp) · legal $30–50K, capped by agreement up front · signature via DocuSign **with wire instructions attached to the signature request** · capital calls → funds landed ≈ 2 weeks. Pre-assembled enhanced room saves ~1 week.
- Round construction (Northflank): a tight seed has the **lead at 80–90%** plus one or two follow-ons the company will actively use; passive cap-table names resurface only to lobby for pro rata. Signaling: a multi-stage fund's seed check that doesn't convert to a lead later reads as "what do insiders know?" — PG's counter: with rising graphs next round, signals don't matter; the mitigation is not taking too much from any one VC.
- Announcement is a decision, not a default (Lenny's): announcing invites competitors to raise and VCs to fund copycats; Qasar Younis delayed announcing his first three rounds by a year each. Test: does announcing address one of the company's three biggest current challenges (hiring, customers, credibility)? If not, it's a distraction.
- Then: existing-investor update cadence starts (`process-and-pipeline.md`), and the next round's target list opens the day after close.
